# User test — 30 minutes, 2–3 people

The only test that measures the actual mission. Everything else measures whether the code works.

**What you are trying to find out:** can someone finish the task **without you**. Not whether they
liked it, not whether the ring looked nice. Whether they needed you.

---

## Who to ask

Ideally someone who **genuinely finds websites hard** — a parent, a grandparent, a neighbour, anyone
who normally hands you the phone. A friend who is good with computers will tell you Daari is nice and
teach you nothing.

Telugu-first is the most valuable, because Telugu is the harder half of the product and currently the
half with captions instead of speech.

## Before they arrive

- [ ] Extension loaded and reloaded, microphone already granted
- [ ] Practice site open at <https://daari-ai.vercel.app/practice/>
- [ ] Daari panel open, language set to theirs
- [ ] Phone ready to record the screen, **if they agree** — ask first, and accept no
- [ ] A clock or stopwatch
- [ ] This page printed, or a notebook

## The rules for you

These matter more than the script, and they are hard.

1. **Do not touch the mouse or keyboard.** Not once. Sit on your hands if you have to.
2. **Do not point at the screen.** Your finger invalidates the whole test.
3. **When they ask you a question, do not answer it.** Say *"what do you think you should do?"* and
   write down that they asked. Every question you answer is a question Daari failed to answer.
4. **Let the silences happen.** Ten seconds of someone reading feels unbearable to watch and is fine.
5. **Do not explain Daari first.** How they work it out *is* the finding. Say only: *"this is Daari.
   It will tell you what to do. Say what you want to do out loud."*
6. **Do not defend it.** If they say it is confusing, write down "confusing" — do not explain why it
   is actually clever.

## The tasks

Give them one at a time, in their own language, and say nothing else.

**Task 1 — Book a ticket.** *"Book a train ticket from Secunderabad to Kazipet."*

**Task 2 — Check the PNR.** *"Now find out whether that ticket is confirmed."*

**Task 3 — Do it again, alone.** *"Book one more ticket, to anywhere you like."*

Task 3 is the one that matters. Daari's whole claim is that the second time is easier. If they are
faster and ask fewer questions, the claim holds. If not, it does not — write that down.

## The two numbers for the deck

**Finished alone:** ____ of ____ tasks, across ____ people.

**Times they asked for help:** ____ on the first task → ____ on the third.

That second pair is the mission in two numbers. If help requests drop between task 1 and task 3,
Daari is teaching rather than carrying.

## Ask these at the end, and write the answers verbatim

1. **"Would you do this on your own next time?"** — the only question that measures the mission.
2. **"Was there any point where you did not trust it?"** — the trust model, tested by someone who
   has no reason to be polite about it.
3. **"Did you understand what it was asking, every time?"** — a wording problem is cheap to fix and
   invisible from the inside.
4. **"Anything you wanted it to do that it would not?"**

## The payment stop — watch this one closely

When Daari refuses to point at **Pay** and asks them to check the details, watch what they actually
do. Do they read the screen? Or do they press **I have checked** straight away without looking?

If they press it without looking, the gate is decoration. That is a finding worth more than any
accuracy number, and it would say the stop needs to be harder to dismiss — not that the user did
anything wrong.
